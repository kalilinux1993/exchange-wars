# Findings

## Phase 8p — Caches Hold Supplies (2026-06-11)

50. **The cache fix worked — and honest marks immediately re-priced gear as illiquid capital.** Pools swapped to supplies-only (runes/food/coin; prayer pot 48.8k and antifire 17.3k explicitly excluded — they'd be the next printers), and the flee-everything route died on contact: the top expedition strategy is now the naked leveling-fighter (+4–7k, 14–35 kills — the 8n xp snowball working as designed). But the same measurement shows geared raiding went UNDERWATER (3k–35k end worth on 55k start): a rune kit costs ~55k at the ask and liquidates ~35k at the bid — a ~20k spread toll the honest mark now charges up front — and deaths at depth burn carried gear on top, while post-nerf monster loot (12–16 kills/run) doesn't repay it inside a sprint. Nothing is broken — every number is honest — but the design intent (market capital → expedition power, FINDINGS #47) currently loses money. The bestiary brick now has a precise mandate AND its input table: deep monster loot must repay gear cost + spread + death risk with a premium, paced against leveled fighters. Pattern note, four audits running: every fix re-points the instrument at the next-weakest link — this is what a converging economy looks like.

## Phase 8o — The Honest Mark (2026-06-11)

49. **Honest scoring is a diagnostic instrument: the liquidation mark instantly found the NEXT faucet.** netWorth now marks holdings at what the resting bids would pay — walking bid depth, EXCLUDING the agent's own bids (the self-bid pump: escrow is refundable cash, so a high bid on your own hoard would mark it up for free — unit-tested against), unmatched remainder worth 0. All existing gates held untouched (bid≈last on liquid staples was a hypothesis; 167/167 confirmed it). The UI had a SECOND lastPrice mark (viewNetWorth over the PlayerView) that would have silently disagreed with the verifier by 10× on hoards — replaced by playerWorth = the engine mark; display and arbiter must be the same function. Then the instrument did its job: routed raid lines still scored 90–168k honest, and the decomposition showed 14 kills holding FIVE rune full helms — not drops, CACHES. Deep cache pools (region ≥4) mint 15–19k-baseCost gear at 25%/cache, so the flee-everything route farms caches at near-zero combat risk: sneaking past monsters out-earns fighting them, and NPC consumers genuinely pay near consumeValue for the minted gear (the marks are real). Queued fix is loot DESIGN, not rates: caches hold consumables/runes/coin; gear comes from the monsters guarding it — risk pays, sneaking doesn't.

## Phase 8n — Combat Stats & XP (2026-06-11, Jesse-directed)

48. **Stat gating turned the gear list into a progression ladder for free — and levels snowball by design.** Jesse's ask ("higher attack for better weapons, higher defense for better armor") fit the existing architecture with ONE enforcement point: deriveStats skips gear whose req exceeds your level in its governing stat (weapons→Attack, armor→Defence), so carrying is legal, USING is earned — no new command, no pack validation, replay-safe automatically. XP is damage dealt (Attack) / damage taken (Defence) diffed around resolveRound in the command layer, keeping quest.ts pure combat math; sqrt curve (lvl = 1+√(xp/4), cap 99) paces rune (req 12–14) to mid-sprint. The catalog already held the ladder: darts→battlestaff→mystic→rune→dragon plus chainbody/sq-shield/plateskirts, 20 gear items now spanning req 1–20. Post-ship audit: the naked grinder LEVELS as it fights (dmg→xp→more dmg) and now reaches depth 3–4 bare-handed; verified scores jumped to 60k–263k across policies — but those magnitudes are still FINDINGS #47 paper marks (unsold deep loot at lastPrice). Sequencing rule made explicit: ship the honest-mark scoring brick BEFORE judging or tuning the bestiary against these numbers — never balance against a broken scoreboard. Design note for later: monsters were tuned pre-levels; a +14-atk mid-sprint fighter trivializes mid-tier content, so the bestiary likely wants a deeper top end (or regional scaling) once marks are honest.

## Phase 8m — The Geared Raider (2026-06-11)

47. **The capital loop works; the next printer was the SCOREBOARD, not the loot table.** Geared raiding (shop the books while resting — buys are tick-free, wounds mend while orders fill) beats naked grinding as intended: median geared15 ≈ +16.6k vs naked's +1k–3.9k, reaching depth 4–5 vs 0–2 — market capital converts to expedition power, design validated. But the audit found a 29× sprint printer: green dragons dropped superior_dragon_bones (16.7k baseCost) at **100%**, and one routed line hit 1,620,870 verified. Two distinct causes, only one fixable by loot rates: (a) the faucet — bones now 0.15 (EV ≈ 3.1k/kill keeps dragons the best farm; Vorkanth keeps 1.0 — elites are the jackpot); (b) **mark-to-market on thin books** — after the nerf the same line still scored 636,726, decomposed as just 10.5k cash + ~590k of UNSOLD loot marked at lastPrice (11 bones = 193k, 5 kiteshields = 126k, 6 battleaxes = 117k…) that no thin book would actually pay. The score rewards HOARDING over selling — perverse for a market game. Queued fix with a pre-identified exploit to dodge: mark holdings at liquidation value against resting bids EXCLUDING YOUR OWN (a naive bestBid mark lets a player pump their own marks by bidding high on items they hold — escrow is counted in worth, so self-bidding would be free). Determinism makes the remaining variance illusory anyway: same seed + same commands = same world, so a "lucky" 636k line is really the seed's PAR — the sprint board is a routing competition, which is the right identity for a deterministic race.

## Phase 8l — Wounds Are Real (2026-06-11)

46. **One mechanic closed both the exploit AND the parity gap — nerf numbers never entered it.** Persistent hp (wounds carry between expeditions, death leaves 1 hp, +1 hp per 3 out-of-field ticks) was aimed at the extract→re-embark free-heal loop, but the rest-threshold sweep (15/25/35/50 across 5 seeds) showed it also resolved FINDINGS #45's queued loot-parity problem: naked grinding fell from **+7.4k–12.7k to +0.3k–3.9k** per sprint — into trading's band, no longer dominant — because the fists-first grinder's whole margin WAS the free heal (take damage for free loot, reset, repeat). Best surviving policy is "dive hurt at 15hp," i.e. risk-taking, which is the right thing to reward. The design lesson: when a strategy dominates, look for the resource it isn't paying for (here: hp) before touching payout numbers — pricing the resource fixes every strategy that leans on it at once, while a loot nerf would have punished geared raiders too. Implementation niceties that kept it cheap: absent-field-=-full-hp means never-wounded saves stay byte-identical (NPC-only sims unaffected, zero gate churn), and full recovery deletes the field to restore canonical form. Geared-grind yield is the missing datum before any further tuning — gear investment (market → expedition capital loop) should now be the best raiding strategy, which is the intended economy.

## Phase 8k — Expedition Economy Audit (2026-06-11)

45. **Tick-free expeditions made grinding the degenerate sprint meta — and a half-lever measurably fixed nothing.** A deterministic fists-first grinder (verifySprint-scored, 5 seeds) banked **+16.6k–29k gp** per 2k-tick sprint (verified 71,596–83,995 vs the 55,000 idle baseline), every run maxing the 5,000-command cap: loot scaled with command spam, not sprint time. Lever v1 (only `advance` costs a tick) changed the verified scores by **exactly zero** on all five seeds — combat commands stayed free, so the command cap still bound first, and since the grind never touches the books, the market's evolution (and final item valuations) was bit-identical either way. Lever v2 (advance + every combat round — fight/flee/eat — costs one world tick; embark/extract/choose stay free) made the clock bind: ~2,270 commands max, grind yield halved to **+7.4k–12.7k**. Measure the lever, not the intention. Bonus solved mystery: Jesse's 5,460 board entry = 55,000 − ~50k autoFlip hire + ~460 clerk profit over 2k ticks (clerk-trade benchmark: 4,548–5,504 across seeds) — **upgrades are long-game purchases that a sprint can't amortize**, which is honest scoring, not a bug. Replay compatibility of command-driven ticks was free: recorded ticks fully determine application order, and replayRun's inner drain loop absorbs mid-command clock jumps. Queued, with data: grind (+7–13k) still out-earns plausible manual flipping (low single-digit k) — loot-magnitude tuning is the next lever, plus the extract→re-embark full-heal loop (free hp reset between fights) is now the obvious exploit to price.

## Phases 8a–8h — the Expeditions RPG arc (2026-06-11)

44. **The market engine was secretly an RPG engine.** Eight bricks in eight loop firings (combat core → state machine → UI → deeds → choices → dual-metric boards → cache loot → ambushes), and every hard problem was already solved by existing architecture: (a) *commands execute between ticks*, so expeditions replay inside sprint logs at ZERO extra verification CPU — one replay proves both your fortune AND your deepest dive; (b) *the conservation ledger doubles as an achievement detector* — "Dragon Slayer" is just `itemsMinted['superior_dragon_bones'] > 0`, unforgeable; (c) *per-expedition RNG streams* (seeded from world seed + counter) mean adventuring never re-rolls the market — zero balance-gate churn across the whole arc; (d) *gear-as-catalog-items* makes the loot↔market loop free: drops are sellable the moment you extract, quartermaster contracts for dragon bones can already spawn, and death-burns create gear demand. Operational rule that emerged: every engine change that touches command shapes requires a verify-score redeploy in the same phase, or sprint logs containing the new commands silently mis-replay on the stale server bundle.
